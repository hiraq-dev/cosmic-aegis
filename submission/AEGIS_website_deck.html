The human challenge

Far from Earth, the body and mind change

  • NASA's Human Research Program names five hazards of spaceflight: radiation, isolation and confinement, distance from Earth, gravity fields, and hostile closed environments.
  • Muscles and bones lose load, sleep and stress shift, and the cardiovascular system adapts.
  • With long communication delays, astronauts need clear, calm self-awareness tools and good information to share with their medical team.
  • Source: nasa.gov/hrp/hazards

    Personal health and the bigger picture

    Not just the mission: the person

    • Personal profile, optional family history and health concerns, all stored only in the browser.
    • Cardiovascular module raises awareness of warning signs. It never diagnoses, predicts or prevents heart attacks.
    • Implemented: forms, validation, charts, rule-based alerts, local storage.
    • Simulated: astronaut, mission, 14-day history, radiation, CO2, cabin temperature.
    • Future: computer-vision exercise analysis, ML health analysis, wearable sensors, clinical validation.

    Technology, NASA resources, collaboration

    Frontend-only prototype, no backend or login

    • React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS, Recharts, Lucide icons; deployed as a static site.
    • Referenced (no datasets or APIs connected): NASA HRP hazards and risks pages, ongoing HRP research, two NASA NTRS evidence reports, two Canadian Space Agency articles.
    • Integration points ready for computer vision, ML analysis and wearables (src/integrations).
    • Space health is a global effort: NASA, CSA, ESA and others share research. Not affiliated with or endorsed by NASA or CSA.
    • Code, text and art created with AI assistance (Claude by Anthropic).
