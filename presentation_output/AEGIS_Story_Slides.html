THE PERSON BEHIND THE MISSION

“I miss home.
And today, I do not feel like myself.”

Imagine Noor, a fictional astronaut on a future deep-space mission.

A family message arrives. She smiles, then notices how tired she feels after exercise.

Her question is simple:

“Has something changed,
and who can help me understand it?”

Isolation and distance make understandable onboard health information valuable.

Fictional scenario, not astronaut testimony. Context: NASA 5 Hazards of Human Spaceflight.2 / 7
THE CHALLENGE

Health information needs context

What Noor experiences

Physical changes during a demanding mission.

Separation from family and familiar support.

Uncertainty when a reading or symptom changes.

What AEGIS aims to provide

A shared view of observations and personal context.

Evidence that the crew can inspect.

A record that supports later medical review.

Challenge fit: gather health indicators and help astronauts evaluate and act on their health status.

Source: Space Apps 2026, Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions.3 / 7

GLOBAL CONNECTION / PROPOSED FRAMEWORK

One crew. A wider circle of care.

Onboard support today.
Shared understanding across agencies tomorrow.

Noor records a concern. A structured case preserves the observation, units, time and source.

A shared health-support framework

Permission-based sharing with authorized medical teams.

Queued handover when communication becomes available.

Common data fields and versioned references for cross-agency review.

Family provides emotional connection. Authorized clinicians review health information.

Future design. No agency integration, partnership or medical-data exchange has been demonstrated.6 / 7
